Free cash flow is the cash a business generates from operations after
paying for the capital expenditures needed to maintain and grow it —
operating cash flow minus capex.

It's the cash actually available to pay down debt, return to shareholders,
or reinvest, and it's harder to distort with accounting choices than net
income, since it's built from cash movements rather than accruals.

Positive free cash flow means the business is self-funding. Negative free
cash flow isn't automatically a red flag — it's expected and often healthy
during a deliberate growth or capex cycle — but it does mean the company is
relying on existing cash, debt, or new equity to fund itself, which is
worth understanding.
