Software and SaaS companies have a cost structure unlike most other
businesses: minimal cost of goods sold (mostly cloud hosting), no physical
inventory, and revenue often collected as cash (subscriptions) ahead of
when it's recognized as earned.

This tends to produce very high gross margins (70-90%+), high current
ratios (large cash balances against modest current liabilities), and free
cash flow that's frequently stronger than net income, since stock-based
compensation and deferred revenue distort net income relative to actual
cash movement.

None of these are inflated or suspicious for this sector — they're
structural.
