Liquidity ratios answer one question: can this company meet its
obligations that come due within roughly the next year, using assets that
are already cash or can become cash quickly?

They say nothing about long-term solvency, profitability, or growth — a
company can be highly liquid and still be shrinking, or tightly liquid and
still be a well-run, cash-up-front business (see the sector exception
notes). Liquidity ratios are best read as a floor check — is there a
near-term funding problem — rather than a measure of overall quality.
