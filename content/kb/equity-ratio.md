Equity ratio is the share of total assets financed by shareholders rather
than by creditors — total equity divided by total assets.

Below 25% means the balance sheet leans heavily on liabilities to fund what
the company owns. 25-50% is typical. Above 50% means shareholders, not
lenders, own most of the asset base — a structurally conservative position.

Unlike debt to equity, this ratio stays meaningful even when equity is
negative: a negative equity ratio is itself a legitimate (and serious) data
point, showing liabilities exceed total assets, rather than becoming
undefined the way debt-to-equity does.
