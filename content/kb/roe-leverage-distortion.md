Return on equity can be inflated by debt rather than genuine efficiency.

Because ROE divides by equity alone, a company that finances more of its
assets with borrowed money — shrinking the equity in the denominator — will
show a higher ROE even if its underlying profitability is unchanged. Two
companies with identical operating performance can post very different ROE
figures purely because one carries more debt.

Always read ROE alongside debt to equity: a high ROE paired with high
leverage is a different, riskier story than a high ROE paired with low
leverage.
