Leverage ratios describe how a company's assets are financed — by
shareholders' equity or by borrowed money — and how much of a cushion
exists before creditors are affected by a downturn.

More leverage magnifies both gains and losses: it can boost returns to
equity holders in good years (see the ROE leverage-distortion note) and
accelerate distress in bad ones. There's no universally "correct" level of
leverage; the right amount depends heavily on how stable and predictable a
company's cash flows are, which is why the sector exception notes matter
more here than in almost any other category.
