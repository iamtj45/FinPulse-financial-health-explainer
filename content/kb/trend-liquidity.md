A liquidity ratio declining over multiple years — even from a comfortable
starting point — is worth more attention than a single low reading.

It can mean current liabilities are growing faster than current assets,
which happens naturally during rapid growth (more payables, more
short-term debt to fund expansion) but can also signal that receivables or
inventory aren't converting to cash as fast as they used to. A single
year's dip during a known event (an acquisition, a large one-time payable)
is less concerning than a multi-year slide with no clear cause.
