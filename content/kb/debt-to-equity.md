Debt to equity compares what a company owes to what its shareholders have
invested.

Below 0.5 is conservative — the balance sheet leans on equity financing,
which carries no fixed repayment obligation. 0.5-1.5 is a common, workable
range for most industries. Above 1.5 means liabilities substantially exceed
equity, which raises fixed obligations that must be met regardless of how
the business performs that year.

This ratio is undefined when equity is negative — dividing by a negative
number produces a mathematically valid but financially meaningless result,
so this tool withholds a number in that case rather than showing one that
would be misread as "very low debt."
