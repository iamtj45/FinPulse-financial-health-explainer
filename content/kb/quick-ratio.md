The quick ratio (also called the acid-test ratio) is the current ratio's
stricter sibling. It removes inventory from current assets before dividing
by current liabilities, because inventory is the current asset most likely
to take time, or a markdown, to convert to cash.

A quick ratio below 0.5 means a company would struggle to cover short-term
obligations without selling inventory or raising cash. 0.5-1.0 is workable
for most industries. Above 1.0 means liquid assets alone cover short-term
liabilities.

Because it strips out inventory, the quick ratio is usually more revealing
than the current ratio for manufacturers and retailers, and nearly identical
to the current ratio for companies that don't carry inventory at all.
