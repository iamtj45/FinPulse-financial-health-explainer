Return on equity measures how much profit a company generates for every
dollar shareholders have invested, net of what's owed to creditors.

Below 5% is weak — shareholders could often do better in a low-risk
alternative. 5-15% is a reasonable, sustainable range for most established
businesses. Above 15% is strong, though it deserves a second look (see the
note on leverage distortion) before assuming it reflects pure operating
efficiency.

ROE is undefined — not simply low — when a company has negative equity,
which is why this tool marks it not meaningful rather than showing a number
in that case.
