Operating cash ratio compares operating cash flow to current liabilities —
a stricter, harder-to-manipulate cousin of the current and quick ratios,
since it uses actual cash generated rather than assets that still need to
be converted to cash.

Below 0.5 means operating cash flow alone would cover less than half of
what's due in the short term. 0.5-1.0 is a reasonable working range. Above
1.0 means a company could cover its current liabilities from operating cash
flow alone, without touching existing cash reserves or selling assets.
