Operating margin takes gross profit and subtracts the operating expenses
required to run the business day to day — R&D, sales, marketing,
administration — before interest and taxes. It answers a narrower question
than net margin: is the core business itself profitable, independent of how
it's financed or taxed.

Below 5% leaves little room for a downturn in demand or a cost spike. 5-15%
is typical for a mature, competitive business. Above 15% usually signals
real pricing power, scale advantages, or both.

A widening gap between gross margin and operating margin over time is worth
watching — it means operating costs are growing faster than the business.
