Grocery and restaurant chains, and much of retail generally, are built
around a cash-conversion cycle where customers pay immediately (or on
delivery) while suppliers are paid on 30-90 day terms.

That structure lets a well-run retailer operate with a current ratio below
1.0 indefinitely — the company is effectively using supplier credit as
working capital rather than sitting on excess liquidity. Gross margins in
this sector are also lower by design (20-35% is typical) than in sectors
that don't resell physical goods bought at wholesale.
