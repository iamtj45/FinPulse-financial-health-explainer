Capital-intensive and asset-backed businesses routinely run debt-to-equity
ratios well above what would be a warning sign elsewhere.

Utilities finance power plants and grids with long-term debt serviced by
predictable, regulated cash flows, and commonly carry ratios of 1.5-2.5 as a
matter of normal operation. Banks and other financial institutions are
structurally leveraged — deposits and borrowed funds are the business — and
conventional debt-to-equity thresholds don't apply to them at all (see the
banking sector note). REITs typically carry significant mortgage debt
against real estate holdings for similar reasons.

A ratio that looks alarming for a software company can be unremarkable for
a utility.
