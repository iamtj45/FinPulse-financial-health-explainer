---
id: negative-equity-explained
tags: [leverage, general]
---

Total equity turns negative when a company's cumulative liabilities exceed
its cumulative assets — often the result of years of losses, large share
buybacks funded by debt, or leveraged transactions like spin-offs.

When equity is negative, return on equity and debt to equity become
mathematically computable but financially meaningless: dividing by a
negative number flips the sign in a way that makes a deeply troubled
company appear to have a suspiciously good-looking ratio. This tool
withholds a number for both ratios in that case and marks them "not
meaningful" rather than showing a misleading figure — the equity ratio,
which divides by total assets instead of equity, remains a meaningful (if
concerning) number in the same situation.
