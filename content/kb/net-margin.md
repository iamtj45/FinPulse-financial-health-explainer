Net margin is what's left of each dollar of revenue after every expense —
cost of goods, operating costs, interest, and taxes. It's the most complete
single-number profitability figure, but also the most exposed to one-off
items that have nothing to do with the ongoing business.

Below 0% means the company lost money over the period. 0-10% is common for
competitive, capital-intensive, or low-margin-by-design businesses (retail,
airlines, contractors). Above 10% suggests either strong pricing power or a
capital-light model.

Because it sits downstream of financing and tax decisions, net margin is
more useful compared against a company's own history than against a
different company's.
