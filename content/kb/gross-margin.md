Gross margin is revenue minus the direct cost of producing what was sold,
divided by revenue. It isolates pricing power and production efficiency
before overhead, R&D, and marketing enter the picture.

A grocery store might run a gross margin near 25%, a hardware retailer near
30-35%, and an enterprise software company north of 75% — none of those
numbers are "better" in isolation, they reflect fundamentally different cost
structures.

What matters more than the absolute level is whether it's stable or
expanding over time; a business that can raise prices or cut production
costs faster than its peers will show it here first.
