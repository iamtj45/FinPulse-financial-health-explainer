FCF margin expresses free cash flow as a percentage of revenue, making it
comparable across companies of different sizes the way a raw free cash flow
dollar figure can't be.

Below 0% means the company burns cash relative to its revenue. 0-10% is a
common range for growing or capital-intensive businesses. Above 10%
indicates the business converts a meaningful share of every revenue dollar
into cash it can freely deploy.

Because it's built on cash flow rather than accounting net income, FCF
margin and net margin can diverge — a company can be net-income profitable
but cash-flow negative, or vice versa, depending on non-cash charges and
working-capital timing.
