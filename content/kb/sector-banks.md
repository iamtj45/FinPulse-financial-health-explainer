Banks and other depository financial institutions report financial
statements in a fundamentally different shape than non-financial companies,
because their business is borrowing and lending money rather than buying
inventory and selling goods at a markup.

Concepts like "current assets," "current liabilities," "inventory," and
"capital expenditure" don't map cleanly onto a bank's balance sheet, so
several ratios in this tool — current ratio, quick ratio, gross margin,
operating margin, free cash flow, FCF margin, and operating cash ratio —
are typically unavailable for banks rather than computed from a forced
approximation. This is a coverage gap, not a data error.

Comparing a bank's available ratios (net margin, ROE, debt to equity,
equity ratio) against a non-financial company's is also not meaningful,
since a bank's leverage and margin structure are unlike any operating
company's by design.
